01

Data Sources

What we measure and where it comes from — Kodo combines a curated hotel directory, daily rate scraping, a statistical occupancy model, and direct operator data partnerships.

Hotel Directory High Confidence

Kodo maintains a manually curated database of {{ public_stats().hotels }}+ branded and independent hotels across {{ public_stats().destinations }} Moroccan destinations. Each property is verified for existence, key count, segment classification, brand affiliation, and ownership. Data quality is flagged at the property level — verified entries have been confirmed against official hotel websites, brand press kits, and industry sources. Unverified entries are clearly marked.

Rate Intelligence Medium Confidence

Since 3 November 2026 (the first full run) Kodo has collected published rates (BAR) every night, from 03:00 Casablanca time, for the hotels in our database that Google Hotels lists, through DataForSEO. Before that date there are no collected prices, so any average or trend over past dates covers only the nights since then.

What is asked for. One request per hotel, for its Google Hotels listing: 2 adults, no children, a one-night stay (check-in tomorrow, check-out the day after), prices in Moroccan dirhams (MAD), results in English, located at the hotel's map position (Morocco as a whole when we have no position), with a price for each check-in date over the next 30 nights. The request does not set a number of rooms, a room type, a board basis or cancellation terms; Google's own defaults apply. Each price is the nightly price Google Hotels returns for that check-in date. A price in another currency is not used, and every result is checked against the hotel's name and city before it is kept.

A published rate is not the hotel's Average Daily Rate (ADR): it excludes negotiated corporate rates, wholesale contracts and package inclusions, and whether taxes and fees are included depends on the booking site. Each price is observed directly, but the history is short, so the source is rated medium for now.

Headline cards. The rate, occupancy and RevPAR cards on hotel, city, brand group and owner pages cover the next 30 nights. Avg published rate averages the latest price collected for each night, at most 7 days old; it is a booking-site price, not ADR. Modelled occupancy comes from the nightly model run. Indicative RevPAR is the published rate times the modelled occupancy, night by night. A city, brand group or owner is the average over its hotels that have the figure, weighted by rooms, and the card shows how many hotels that covers. A change against 7 days earlier appears once 7 days of figures exist. The yearly occupancy, ADR and RevPAR still shown in some tables (FY 2025) are Kodo model estimates, not reported data, and are labelled that way.

Occupancy Model Low Confidence

Kodo does not have access to real occupancy data from hotel PMS systems except for properties participating in our Data Sharing Program. For all other properties occupancy is modelled, not measured. Two versions of the model run every night; the platform shows one of them. Its weights are set by hand and have not yet been checked against real figures, and prices have been collected only since 3 November 2026, so the source is rated low.

Version 1 starts from a base occupancy for the hotel's segment and type (city, resort, leisure, riad or camp), and adjusts it for the day of the week, the month, a short list of events, and the night's published rate compared with the hotel's estimated average rate.

Hotel type. A hotel with fewer than 30 rooms counts as a riad or boutique hotel. A name such as Riad, Dar, Kasbah, Palais, Lodge or Camp also makes it one, but only up to 40 rooms: riads typically have 20 to 30. Larger hotels take their type from their city: beach resort, leisure city or business city. Kasbahs and camps in desert and small towns (Ouarzazate, Zagora, Merzouga, Errachidia) count as leisure. A few hotels have their type set by hand after a check.

Version 2 keeps the same base, day-of-week and monthly pattern, and replaces the rest with signals read from the market every night:

  • The hotel's normal price. Its own median published price for each day of the week over the last 60 days. A hotel with fewer than 14 priced nights uses its estimated average rate instead, and its range is wider.
  • Market heat. For each city and night, how far prices are above or below normal across all hotels with a price (by segment when at least 5 hotels have one). Hotels whose price barely moves count less. When a whole city prices up, demand is likely up; one hotel's own move counts too, but less.
  • Nights that disappear. When a night's price vanishes from a lookup that otherwise worked, while the hotel has prices on the nights around it, the night may be sold out. It counts as sold out only if this shows in two lookups in a row. A hotel with no prices for 5 nights or more in a row is treated as closed or unknown, not sold out, and a failed lookup is never read as sold out. Sold-out hotels in a city raise the estimate for the others.
  • Calendar. Moroccan public and religious holidays (Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, Throne Day and the others), Moroccan school holidays (public schools and French-curriculum schools in Morocco), and events held in Morocco, for 2026 and 2027, with different effects for city, resort, leisure and riad hotels. The model also allows for school-holiday periods in visitors' home countries; these are not shown in the events calendar. Islamic holidays in 2027 are estimates until announced.

Version 2 gives a range (for example 62–74%), not a single figure. The range is wider when few hotels in the city have prices that night, when the hotel has a short price history, and when there is no price for the night; it is narrower when a sold-out night is confirmed. The range is a judgement of how much the signals agree, not a measured margin of error. All weights are set by hand for now and will be fitted once hotels share real figures.

What it cannot know. Kodo never sees bookings: group and corporate blocks, events not in the calendar, how many rooms a hotel actually has for sale, or why a price changed (a promotion looks like weak demand). A published price is not the rate guests paid. A hotel whose price never changes gives almost no signal. A sold-out night can also be a night the hotel stopped selling online.

Price coverage (full, partial or none) describes how many recent published prices we have for the hotel, not how accurate the estimate is: none with no published rate for the night, partial with one, full with one and rates collected for the hotel on at least 4 of the last 7 days. Neither version uses "rooms left" messages.

Verified Operator Data Highest Confidence

Hotels participating in the Kodo Data Sharing Program share daily operational data (occupancy, ADR, RevPAR) directly from their Property Management System. It is the hotel's own reported data, so it is the most reliable source when a hotel shares it. This data is anonymised before inclusion in any benchmarking output and is never disclosed at the individual property level. It is compared with the model's estimates to measure the model's error, and will be used to fit the model's weights. (The "Verified" badge on a hotel profile means the hotel's directory entry has been checked, not that it shares data.)

Tourism Data and the 2027 Kodo Forecast

The Tourism screen shows official figures only, each stored with its source, its publication date, the period it covers and whether it is final or provisional. The 2026 estimate and the 2027 figures are Kodo’s own forecast, drawn in a separate style and never presented as official.

  • Arrivals are arrivals at border posts and include Moroccans resident abroad (MRE). The official table by nationality counts MRE under their country of residence, so “France” there is far larger than foreign French tourists alone. From 2025 the official figures no longer split foreign tourists and MRE.
  • Nights and occupancy cover classified tourist accommodation (EHTC) only, not all accommodation. The official series was widened: 2019 and 2023 onwards are on the current basis, 2020–2022 only on the previous, narrower one (2024: 39.9 million nights on the current basis, 28.7 million on the previous). Only the current basis is shown; occupancy is shown for 2024 and 2025, the years published on that basis.
  • Travel receipts are the balance-of-payments travel receipts of the Office des Changes, in dirhams at current prices; the latest year is provisional.
  • Airport passengers are the airport authority’s figures as reported in the press, because its own releases could not be read; they are marked as secondary.
  • All figures are calendar years. 2020 and 2021 are marked as COVID years.

How the Kodo forecast is made. First, 2026 is estimated from the latest year-to-date figures. Asset Value Estimation

Kodo estimates indicative asset values using revenue multiples by segment — a standard methodology in hotel real estate. These multiples represent the relationship between annual rooms revenue and total asset value, calibrated against available Morocco transaction data and regional benchmarks.

Segment Revenue Multiple Range Commentary
Ultra Luxury 18 – 22× Trophy assets, limited transactions
Luxury 15 – 18× Strong institutional demand
Upper Upscale 12 – 15× Most liquid segment
Upscale 10 – 12× Growing investor interest
Midscale 8 – 10× Value-add profile
Economy 6 – 8× Operator-driven market

Disclaimer: These multiples are indicative only and should not be used as the sole basis for investment decisions. Hotel asset values are highly sensitive to operator quality, lease structure, capex requirements, and market conditions. Contact Kodo Advisory for asset-specific valuation.

05

Understanding Our Data Quality Labels

Every property and metric on the Kodo platform carries a data quality label. These are not cosmetic — they indicate the source and reliability of the underlying data.

Verified

Data confirmed against official sources — brand press kits, official hotel websites, government tourism records, or direct operator communication. Key count, opening year, brand affiliation, and ownership have been individually verified.

Estimated

Performance metrics (occupancy, ADR, RevPAR) estimated using our statistical model. Suitable for market-level analysis and relative benchmarking. Not verified against operator PMS data. Always labelled clearly in platform displays and reports.

Key Count Uncertain

Hotel existence is confirmed but the precise room count has not been independently verified. Key count shown is our best estimate based on available sources including brand materials, planning records, and press coverage.

06

What We Don't Claim

Transparency about limitations is a core part of our methodology. The following points should be understood by all users of Kodo data.

  • Kodo estimates are not a substitute for operator-verified PMS data. For investment underwriting, always seek actual operator performance data.
  • BAR (Best Available Rate) is not equivalent to ADR (Average Daily Rate). BAR excludes negotiated corporate rates, loyalty redemptions, wholesale contracts, and package inclusions, which can represent 30–60% of a hotel's actual revenue mix.
  • The error of the occupancy model has not been measured yet: no hotel has shared enough real figures to test it. Once they do, Kodo compares both versions of the model with those figures every month, by city and hotel type, and will publish the result. Until then, treat modelled occupancy as a direction, not a figure.
  • Financial estimates — asset values, revenue ranges, and RevPAR figures — are indicative only. They reflect current market conditions and available data, and may differ materially from actual performance or appraised values.
  • All data on the Kodo platform should be independently verified before forming the basis of investment decisions, operator negotiations, or financial reporting.
  • Kodo Hospitality is a market intelligence provider, not a licensed financial advisor. Nothing on this platform constitutes investment advice.
07

Improving Accuracy Through Partnership

The accuracy of Kodo's market intelligence improves with every hotel that contributes real operational data. Properties in our Data Sharing Program share daily occupancy, ADR, and RevPAR from their PMS. This data is anonymised and aggregated — individual hotel figures are never disclosed to other subscribers.

In return, participating hotels receive free Benchmarker access — our full competitive intelligence suite including anonymous comp set benchmarking, trend charts, and monthly summaries — at no cost, for as long as the data sharing agreement is active. Data contributors also benefit from improved model accuracy for their own market, better seasonal calibration, and early access to new platform features.

What participants receive:

  • Free Benchmarker plan access (normally MAD 2,490/month)
  • Anonymous comp set benchmarking vs. your competitive set
  • Monthly performance trend reports
  • Priority support and direct access to our research team
  • Influence over coverage expansion and new features

Data is processed under a strict confidentiality agreement. Individual property data is never disclosed in any form. Aggregated benchmarking output always includes a minimum of 3 properties per disclosure, per STR confidentiality standards.
